# OctoAcme Project Management Docs

OctoAcme manages cross-functional projects through initiation, planning, execution, release, and retrospectives. The approach emphasizes customer value, iterative delivery, clear ownership, data-informed decisions, and psychological safety. Initiation validates the problem, outcomes, success measures, stakeholders, and team availability before a go/no-go decision. Planning turns approved work into prioritized, estimated increments with acceptance criteria, milestones, a Definition of Done, and risk and dependency tracking.

Project Managers coordinate delivery, schedules, risks, and communication. Product Managers define customer and business outcomes, prioritize the backlog, and measure results. Developers estimate and deliver features, tests, and documentation; QA validates quality and acceptance criteria; stakeholders provide input and approvals. Shared artifacts, including the project one-pager, backlog, risk register, and release plan, keep responsibilities and decisions visible.

During execution, teams track work on a project board, hold regular standups and delivery syncs, and demonstrate progress at sprint or milestone reviews. PM and Product Manager alignment and regular stakeholder updates maintain transparency, with a project README or release document serving as the single source of truth for status. Risks have owners and mitigation plans and are reviewed regularly. Blockers escalate from the team through the PM and Product Lead to the sponsor when necessary.

Quality practices include small, reviewed pull requests, automated tests and linting in CI, applicable integration tests, security scanning, critical-flow smoke tests, and manual QA when needed. Releases require satisfied acceptance criteria, passing CI and security checks, release notes, and rollback plans, followed by staging smoke tests, production verification, and stakeholder announcements. Retrospectives after sprints, releases, milestones, and incidents turn feedback into prioritized improvement actions with owners, due dates, and measurable success criteria.
